questions per language: four synonyms and three antonyms in English, and four
kasingkahulugan and three kasalungat in Filipino. This allocation supersedes any
assumption that every supplied reference item requires an output for this batch.
The images are conceptual reference material; highlighted keys are not authority.
No source images or source sentences are reproduced in the distributable corpus.

The new sentences use the selected vocabulary targets in independently authored
situations. Keeping a target word preserves the vocabulary knowledge being tested;
it does not reuse the source's distinctive sentence, distractor set, or explanation.

is required. No visual or shared reference is needed.

## Editorial and lexical review

Provisional difficulty: 8 easy, 6 medium, no hard items. English has 3 easy and
4 medium; Filipino has 5 easy and 2 medium. Labels reflect expected
lexical familiarity, sense selection, and distractor demand; an uncommon word is
not automatically hard. Context hints are intentional for learner-friendly
practice. These estimates require maintainer review and later empirical evidence.

Every explanation identifies the intended sense and explains why other choices
do not fit. Antonyms contrast the selected sense rather than claiming a universal
opposite across every meaning. Thus `yumabong` refers to flourishing trade, while
`pagdaong` contrasts with departure from the same port.

Source corrections/selection safeguards:

- The source's highlighted antonym "well-thought" for candid is unsuitable for
  the frank/honest sense. The new item uses an openly truthful account versus
  an evasive account. See [Cambridge's candid entry](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/candid).
- `Nauulinigan` describes hearing the radio indistinctly; the explanation retains
  that nuance instead of equating it with complete understanding. The consulted
  [ulinig entry](https://diksiyonaryo.ph/search/ulinig) describes something heard
  but not understood. This is a root-level lexical reference, not an inflection guide.
- `Tigib` uses the full/filled sense, not its separate tool meaning. See the
  [tigib entry](https://diksiyonaryo.ph/search/tigib).
- Boulevard/alley is not used as a strict antonym pair. The unclear source
  matimyas/di-totoo pairing is also not selected. Source keys were not imported.

Definitions and examples here are original explanatory prose, not quotations of
dictionary examples. Source access does not establish source-image redistribution
rights. Bilingual editorial review, originality review, reviewed revision/checksum,
and maintainer sign-off remain pending. This is draft content only: no import,
publication, question lifecycle, schema change, or deployment occurs.

## Editorial corrections — corpus revision 9

English 03 now asks for the closest meaning of EPHEMERAL. It no longer asks for
literal substitution that would leave an incorrect article before "short-lived."

All six antonym items now mix distractors representing different semantic
confusions instead of three synonyms surrounding one obvious outlier. English
items distinguish manner from hesitation/accuracy, frankness from detail/bias,
and happiness from surprise/anxiety. Filipino items distinguish growth from
continuation/change, sadness from uncertainty/anticipation, and docking from
voyaging/sinking. Each retains one clearly best contextual opposite, its existing
key, and an explanation addressing the revised distractors. None relies on
displayed option letters or positions.

Filipino 01 and 03 are now easy: the radio context directly cues hearing, and the
conflicting instructions directly cue confusion. Their existing learner-friendly
contexts and choices are retained. These editorial changes do not add questions
or alter the seven-per-language, four-synonym/three-antonym allocation. All nine
affected questions increment to version 2, and the corpus increments to revision 9.
Maintainer bilingual review and sign-off remain pending.
